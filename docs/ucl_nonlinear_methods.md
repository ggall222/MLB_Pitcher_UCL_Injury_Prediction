# Nonlinear UCL model evaluation: methods and limits

## Unchanged study and isolated artifacts

The complete `ucl_dynamic` endpoint, appearance risk set, chronological splits, 60-day embargo, prior-history rules, active-episode exclusions and first-event competing injury policy are reused through its existing helpers. No source database object is created or altered. Every prior report, notebook, model and analysis source module is hashed before work and checked at completion. Outputs are isolated under `reports/ucl_nonlinear`, `data/injury/analysis/ucl_nonlinear` and `notebooks/ucl_nonlinear`.

Focus on the existing primary 30-day landmark outcome, rather than multiplying searches across four horizons. Evaluate warning at 7–30, 14–30 and 21–30 days. Training ends in 2021, validation is 2022–2023, transfer is the already examined 2024–2025. No evaluation uses incomplete 2026 observations. Neither validation nor transfer is independently untouched after repeated project development.

The primary endpoint is retrospectively source-documented UCL, not all elbow injury or exact biological onset. Known non-UCL first injuries are competing negatives; uncertain possible UCL/arm episodes censor a row. Regular negatives require observed follow-up under the existing rule. Current-outing predictors are available only after completion. Diagnosis, procedure and activation information never enter the new predictor blocks. Pitchers may recur across chronological periods; whole-pitcher resampling handles repeated-observation uncertainty, not complete unseen-player independence.

## Models and restrained search

Seven ordered feature blocks are copied unchanged from the frozen dynamic stage. The prior explicit profile×departure interaction model is not used as the linear benchmark: the same-feature additive L2 comparator is used for each representation. Existing fitted L2 models are loaded directly, and exact validation/transfer AP and Brier equivalence is checked.

The additive spline model is a logistic GAM-like basis expansion, not a separately fitted smoothing-penalty package. For each continuous source feature, cubic B-splines use up to four distinct training quantile knots. At least eight distinct transformed values and three distinct knot positions are required; otherwise that field remains linear. Indicator, discrete and low-support columns remain linear. Constant extrapolation prevents unbounded tails; source features retain the existing train-only 0.5%/99.5% caps, median imputation and missingness indicators. Basis columns are standardized on training and logistic coefficients receive L2 regularization at C=0.1 or 1. There are no tensor products or interaction terms. This is an additive nonlinear comparison, not an automatically discovered interaction model. See the installed-version [SplineTransformer documentation](https://scikit-learn.org/1.6/modules/generated/sklearn.preprocessing.SplineTransformer.html).

Histogram gradient boosting uses the same imputed/capped original features and indicators; it is not allowed a different missingness advantage. Fixed settings are 150 iterations, learning rate 0.05, minimum leaf size 200, L2=10, no class weights and leaf/depth combinations 2/1, 4/2 and 8/3. Two-leaf depth-one stumps remain additive on log-odds. Four/eight-leaf trees permit interactions. Early stopping is explicitly disabled because an automatic random internal validation split would violate the chronological design; iteration count is fixed before fitting. See [HistGradientBoostingClassifier](https://scikit-learn.org/1.6/modules/generated/sklearn.ensemble.HistGradientBoostingClassifier.html). No SMOTE, class rebalancing, outcome-driven feature selection or neural networks are used.

There are 35 nonlinear candidate fits, plus seven reused logistic benchmarks. Every setting is fit using training only. The primary selection rule is validation 7–30d all-event recall at a 5% retrospective appearance budget, requiring AP above prevalence. Ties prefer fewer raw fields, simpler setting, then AP; identifiers break remaining ties deterministically. Select one setting per algorithm/representation and one global warning leader per algorithm. Separately freeze the validation AP leader per algorithm as a distinct ranking hypothesis; do not conflate it with the warning leader. Both objectives are declared before evaluation. Settings, source signatures and 95th-percentile validation thresholds are written before any new transfer scores. There is no train+validation refit or transfer-based revision.

## Evaluation

Reuse the existing rank metrics and first-next-event warning function unchanged. Report AP, PR lift, ROC-AUC, Brier, calibration bias and validation-quantile calibration tables. Report 1/2.5/5/10% retrospective budgets, all-event and observable-event recall, ≥7/14/21d warnings, each event's first-warning lead, alert count, alerts per detected event, and false/nonactionable burden. No alert skips over a competing injury to claim a later UCL event. Lead-time medians condition on detection; denominators include unobservable events. Labeled false alerts refer to the first-event UCL estimand, not evidence that a pitcher had no medical issue.

Numeric thresholds are learned from validation scores only and applied unchanged in transfer, with realized alert rates. All-prospectively-eligible-appearance burden also reports unknown-outcome alerts rather than declaring censored rows healthy. Calibration is diagnostic; no post-hoc calibrator is fit. Near-baseline Brier scores can coexist with poor discrimination for rare outcomes and do not demonstrate clinical calibration.

Conditional uncertainty uses the existing 300 paired whole-pitcher bootstrap utility for AP, and whole-pitcher event bootstrap for warning recall. Scores and budget cutoffs are fixed in this conditional bootstrap. Eight training refits use the same pitcher draws across selected models, with fixed hyperparameters and training-fitted transformations rebuilt each time. These refits assess composition sensitivity, not a new tuning search. Hyperparameter-selection uncertainty is not fully incorporated. Empirical intervals can be degenerate when no event is detected; a zero range is not a population bound.

## Effects and interaction interpretation

A fixed training reference takes at most five outings per pitcher, then at most 4,000 rows, using a fixed seed without consulting targets. This reduces long-career dominance in effect plots; it is not the original prevalence-weighted evaluation population. One-dimensional ALE uses ten bins within the observed central 98% of this reference. Each bin averages changes in predicted log-odds when its observed feature is moved between its two boundaries, holding that row's other inputs fixed. Cumulative local changes are centered with bin occupancy weights. Missing source values are not imputed into the plotted observed-value distribution. Empty bins abort a curve rather than inventing support.

ALE reduces the broad extrapolation of marginal partial dependence under correlated features, but still describes the fitted model, not causal physiology. The implementation follows the local-difference idea of [Apley and Zhu, *Visualizing the Effects of Predictor Variables in Black Box Supervised Learning Models*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.08468). Shading spans the minimum and maximum across eight whole-pitcher refits on the same reference; it is not a 95% confidence interval. Plot bin counts, pitcher counts, and numeric curves are retained.

The interaction diagnostic is **not full second-order ALE**. It averages the local four-corner mixed contrast `f(high,high) − f(high,low) − f(low,high) + f(low,low)` on log-odds within 4×4 training quantile rectangles. Arbitrary additive main effects cancel, including curved GAM effects. Cells require at least 40 reference rows and 15 pitchers; others are masked. Local corners can still imply model perturbations in poorly supported directions within a rectangle. Weighted RMS contrasts, refit map correlations and sign agreement describe fitted nonadditivity, not causal interaction or incremental predictive utility.

Predeclared velocity/release, extension, baseline×departure and workload×departure probes are evaluated where frozen representations contain both fields. Supplemental profile-only pairs (baseline velocity×extension; baseline release-Z variability×age) provide interpretation coverage for the profile AP leader. They do not alter selection and are explicitly exploratory. Selected profile+departure/previous-62 stump models are useful additive controls: their log-odds mixed contrasts must be zero even when their main effects are nonlinear. Unit tests verify both additive nulls and a synthetic known product.

Marginal source-feature permutation on validation is descriptive: values and their missingness move together through the fitted encoder. Three repetitions assess the original fit; one repetition per training refit assesses importance stability for the planned probes. Permutation may break feature correlations and temporal relationships and is not conditional or causal importance. Correlated original/derived measures can dilute or redistribute importance. Features are not selected or removed based on this diagnostic.

## Influence and limits

AP is decomposed by assigning each positive row its tie-aware precision-threshold contribution divided by the number of positives, then summing by injury episode. Fractions sum to one, but AP is not additive under deletion because ranks change. Therefore also remove each injured pitcher, including all their positive and negative rows, and recompute AP. This leave-one-pitcher-out score diagnostic does not retrain or select a different model. It asks whether apparent gains depend on a small number of pitchers. High-influence profiles retain training percentiles and prior-appearance support for review.

Role and seen/unseen-pitcher subgroup diagnostics use subgroup observable-event denominators, clearly separated from primary all-event recall. They are too small to claim subgroup generalization. Rare events, endpoint uncertainty, later surgical confirmation, historical tracking coverage, missing direct biomechanics and repeated exploratory validation constrain interpretation. Better flexible fitting, large local contrasts or attractive effect curves do not prove useful UCL warning, tissue damage, physiological thresholds or clinical readiness.
