# Suggested figure captions

All four figures use the established **broad Elbow / UCL** outcome, including nonspecific elbow diagnoses. They must not be described as confirmed-UCL-only results. The evaluated periods have already been examined; these are exploratory findings.

**Figure 1. Matched pre-event supervised-component trajectories.** Frozen training-derived PLS scores for departure (D1–D2) and combined profile/departure (C1–C2) inputs, aligned to the final five observed outings before the recorded injury/IL event or matched control anchor. The same 62 event pairs (60 injured pitchers; 54 connected pair clusters) contribute to every point. Only 4 cases explicitly name UCL injury at initial placement. Axes are oriented by the training logistic coefficients and expressed in training-score SD units. Shading is a joint 95% bootstrap band across all seven retained axes and five positions, separately within cases and controls; annotations give paired last-minus-first separation changes with descriptive 95% intervals. D1 change is +0.12 [-0.36, +0.63] SD and C1 is +0.08 [-0.20, +0.39] SD. These contrasts do not establish a common approaching-injury rise. The last outing is a median 1 day before the recorded event, so late separation would not itself demonstrate a seven-day warning.

**Figure 2. Supervised-component separation by lead time.** Equal-event-weighted case-minus-control differences for all validation-retained departure and combined PLS axes, in training component SD units on a shared zero-centered color scale. Outing alignment uses 62 complete matched pairs; calendar alignment uses a different set of 32 pairs, complete in all four bins. Calendar values are event/group means within actual elapsed-day bins. A dot would indicate that a joint 95% bootstrap interval excludes zero; 0 of 63 cells meet that criterion. Joint bands span all components and positions within each alignment. Color magnitude is not predictive importance, and these means do not estimate horizon-specific discrimination or biological onset.

**Figure 3. PCA versus PLS validation compression.** Average Precision versus retained continuous dimensions for the original 62 inputs, departure inputs, and combined profile/departure inputs. All transforms and classifiers were trained through 2021. Curves reuse 26,796 validation appearances from 2022–2023 (600 positive rows; 89 represented broad elbow/UCL events). Squares mark the prior validation-selected component counts; dashed lines show unreduced models using the same panel's inputs and dotted lines show prevalence. PLS selects 10 versus 30 PCA axes for original inputs, 2 versus 8 for departure, and 5 versus 20 for combined inputs. Context and missingness columns bypass projection, so latent dimension is not total model size. Selection-period estimates are optimistic; the curve supports compact ranking but does not demonstrate a statistically clear practical-warning improvement.

**Figure 4. Individual matched-event departure trajectories.** D1 scores for 62 individual elbow/UCL events, their matched controls, and paired differences across the final five outings. D1 was selected by its training standardized classifier weight, not the displayed trajectories, and is dominated by lower fastball-velocity departure. All panels share event order, sorted by case score at the final outing minus the average at the first two outings. Scores retain a common training-derived origin and SD without per-event centering. Colors clip at ±3 SD; exported cells retain exact unclipped values and the row lookup supplies event identities. Case scores increase under this descriptive contrast in 40/62 events; controls increase in 26/62. Rising, falling, persistently different, and irregular paths coexist. Sorting creates a gradient by construction and does not identify injury phenotypes or establish an alert threshold.
